Where the Service shades regions of a body diagram, that shading is a relative visual index of your own data and nothing else. 6. How Dose Figures Are Produced

You are entitled to know exactly where the numbers come from, because it determines how little weight they can bear.

A. The default is a typical value, not your value

Unless you enter your own procedure parameters, every figure in your history is a published typical or representative dose for a procedure of that general type — not a measurement of what you received.

That is a deliberate product decision. Most people know they had “a CT of the head” and nothing more, and demanding contrast phases, scanner models, and dose-length products would produce an empty history that is worth nothing. So the app defaults to the most commonly performed variant and its typical published value, and lets you log in one tap.

The consequence is that a default figure can be off by a wide margin — potentially by a factor of several — from the dose you actually received. Real doses vary with the scanner, the protocol, the operator, the facility, your body size and habitus, the number of phases or acquisitions, the contrast used, the year the study was performed, and much else. None of that is known to the app unless you supply it.

B. Optional refinement

If you hold a dose report or other paperwork, you may enter specific parameters — such as dose-length product, CTDIvol, kerma-area product, administered activity, or prescribed dose — and the app will use those instead. This generally produces a figure closer to your actual exposure, but it does not make the figure correct, verified, or clinically usable. Conversion coefficients are themselves published approximations that embed assumptions about anatomy and technique which may not hold for you.

C. Estimated entries

Some catalog entries carry values that are interpolated or estimated rather than drawn from a specific published figure, and some values remain pending verification against their primary sources. Where the app can express uncertainty — as a range or a stated uncertainty on a record — that expression is part of the disclosure and should be read as such. An entry shown without a range is not thereby more reliable.

D. Aggregation

Cumulative totals are the arithmetic sum of the figures described above. Summing approximations does not produce a measurement, and a total carries all of the uncertainty of its parts. The Service’s separation of different categories of dose exists for record-keeping tidiness and is not a clinical or dosimetric standard.
